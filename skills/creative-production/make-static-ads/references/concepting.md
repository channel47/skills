# Concepting

Use this reference only when creative concepts are not already approved.

## Distill the problem

Express the assignment in five lines:

1. Audience and awareness level
2. Desired action
3. Landing-page or offer promise
4. Strongest mechanism, evidence, or customer truth
5. Claims and production constraints

Do not concept from a product description alone. Understand what the audience already believes, what interrupts them, and what the destination must fulfill.

## Diverge across strategic territories

Explore relevant territories, not a mandatory checklist:

- pain or frustration;
- desired outcome;
- unexpected mechanism;
- comparison or replacement;
- demonstration or proof;
- challenge or test;
- identity or aspiration;
- curiosity or pattern interruption;
- objection resolution;
- social proof or authority when approved evidence exists.

Vary the visual mechanism independently:

- action photograph;
- transformation;
- split comparison;
- product-format reveal;
- checklist or challenge card;
- editorial statement;
- evidence-led layout;
- deliberately product-absent prospecting image;
- tactile macro detail;
- annotated real-world scene.

Also vary message density where strategically useful:

- **Copy-free** — the image itself carries the hook; action comes from the platform button or caption.
- **Light-copy** — one short label, claim, or curiosity line supports the image.
- **Type-led** — headline, proof, offer, or CTA is central to the visual idea.

Do not force every concept into the same headline-plus-CTA template. A product still life, action image, transformation, or visual joke can be a complete ad when the composition communicates the intended hypothesis without explanation.

Reject concepts that differ only by headline, color, crop, or product position.

## Build a testable portfolio

For each candidate, state:

- the audience insight;
- the strategic hypothesis;
- the visual stopping mechanism;
- the landing-page continuity;
- what makes it distinct from the other candidates.

Score 1–5:

| Criterion | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Stopping power | Does the visual interrupt attention without relying on noise? |
| Comprehension | Is the proposition understood within roughly two seconds? |
| Relevance | Does it dramatize a real audience problem or desire? |
| Congruence | Will the click feel continuous with the destination? |
| Believability | Does it remain credible and evidence-safe? |
| Feasibility | Can it be generated faithfully with available references? |
| Scalability | Can the concept survive all required placements? |

Do not choose only the highest arithmetic scores. Preserve distinct hypotheses so the test can teach something.

## Expand production candidates

Usually expand the strongest 3–5. Include:

- **Concept name**
- **Strategic angle**
- **Composition**
- **Copy mode and exact on-image copy, or none**
- **CTA treatment: on-image, platform button, caption, or none**
- **Why it may work**
- **Production and claims risks**
- **Recommended pilot format**

Generate one pilot when the direction is clear. Generate two pilots from different strategic territories when the human needs to compare production methods or visual approaches.
