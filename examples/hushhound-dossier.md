## Stage: Personas

### The Burned Buyer (primary)
Solution-aware · veteran of 3+ failed fixes · converts on proof-from-peers + risk reversal.

> "My neighbor left a note about the barking. Again. I've done the YouTube methods, the $200 trainer, the citronella collar — each worked for about a week (Q3, Q12). So I'm googling reviews at midnight, but I don't believe any of these things work anymore. Show me a dog as bad as mine (Q18)."

| Lead with | The decay pattern: "worked for a week, then..." |
| Prove with | Unpolished before/after video from a multi-failure owner + 30-day refund |
| Avoid | Polished brand aesthetics; any "instant results" claim (Q18, Q27) |
| CTA style | Risk-reversal: "30 days. If it doesn't work for YOUR dog, full refund." |
| Best platform | Meta video → branded-search retargeting |
| Hook archetype | Empathy + failed-solution |

### Anti-persona: The Force-Free Purist
Philosophically opposed to any deterrent device; no safety data changes a values position; comments hostile things on ads, raising CAC (Q41). Recognize by "R+ only" / "force-free" language in targeting overlaps. Exclude, don't persuade.

## Stage: Angles

### Angle 1 — "The One-Week Wonder" (Tier 1)
Failed-solution + discovery · The Burned Buyer · solution-aware · anchors: Q3, Q12, Q29

1. "The citronella collar worked great — for exactly one week."
2. "Your dog isn't stubborn. Your last three fixes all made the same mistake."
3. "$400 in trainers and collars later, the thing that worked cost less than the leash."

**Failure mode**: naming competitor brands invites legal review — keep it to categories ("the collar", "the trainer"). Doesn't attract the anti-persona (no shock/spray framing), but avoid the word "deterrent" in copy.

### Angles to avoid
